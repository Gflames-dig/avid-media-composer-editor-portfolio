## Representative Editing Workflows

The following case studies show how I approach common editing assignments. They are workflow demonstrations rather than claims of additional named projects or client results.

### 1. Event or Service Highlight Video

**Objective:** Create a concise video that communicates the atmosphere, central moments, and key message of a service or event.

**Approach:**

1. Clarify the audience, intended platform, approximate duration, and key moments that must be included.
2. Review and organize available media, then identify useful establishing shots, reactions, speaker moments, and supporting visuals.
3. Build a coherent first assembly in Avid Media Composer, arranging selected footage into a beginning, middle, and ending.
4. Refine the pacing and transitions so the sequence feels purposeful rather than like a collection of unrelated clips.
5. Review continuity, audio/visual consistency, titles where needed, and the relationship between the footage and the intended message.
6. Complete a final playback and export check before delivery.

**Quality focus:** A clear story, purposeful shot selection, balanced pacing, consistent presentation, and a final edit that respects the event and its audience.

### 2. Long-Form Message or Teaching Video

**Objective:** Make a longer recorded message easier to follow while preserving the speaker's meaning and the integrity of the content.

**Approach:**

- Identify the main message and the natural structure of the recording before editing.
- Preserve statements and context needed for the message to remain accurate and complete.
- Tighten avoidable pauses or distractions where appropriate without making the speaker feel unnatural or changing the intended meaning.
- Maintain continuity between sections and review the full sequence for abrupt edits or distracting changes.
- Check intelligibility, synchronization, titles or section labels where needed, and the complete export.

**Quality focus:** Message integrity, continuity, intelligibility, and a viewing experience that supports understanding rather than distracting from the content.

### 3. Short Promotional or Announcement Edit

**Objective:** Deliver a short, focused video that gives viewers a clear reason to pay attention and understand the next step.

**Approach:**

- Open with a strong and relevant visual or message.
- Prioritize the information viewers need most; remove material that does not support the objective.
- Maintain energetic but readable pacing, making sure the edit does not sacrifice clarity for speed.
- Keep titles, dates, names, and calls to action consistent with the approved brief.
- Review the final version at full length and confirm that the intended message remains clear without additional explanation.

**Quality focus:** Immediate clarity, concise storytelling, readable on-screen information, and consistency with the communication goal.

## My Editing Workflow

### 1. Understand the brief

Confirm the audience, purpose, required content, tone, duration, delivery format, deadline, and approval process.

### 2. Organize and review media

Keep source material and project assets organized, review footage systematically, and identify the clips most relevant to the story.

### 3. Build the sequence

Create an initial structure in Avid Media Composer, then refine the timeline through selection, trimming, pacing, and continuity decisions.

### 4. Refine the edit

Review the piece from the viewer's perspective. Check that the story flows, cuts are purposeful, and audio, visuals, titles, and message support each other.

### 5. Review and deliver

Incorporate agreed feedback, check file and version naming, play the export from beginning to end, and confirm that the delivered version meets the brief.

## Quality-Control Checklist

Before considering an edit ready, I review:

- **Story:** Does the sequence communicate the intended message and make sense to its audience?
- **Continuity:** Are the cuts, transitions, and timing coherent?
- **Audio-visual consistency:** Is the sound understandable, and does it stay aligned with the picture?
- **Titles and details:** Are names, dates, labels, and other on-screen information correct and readable?
- **Presentation:** Are visual choices consistent with the brief and the content?
- **Technical delivery:** Does the export play correctly, use the agreed format, and carry the correct filename/version?
- **Final review:** Has the entire exported video been checked rather than only selected points?
